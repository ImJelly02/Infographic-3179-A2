01. The Origins

Mapping the Displacement

Malaysia continues to be a destination for displaced communities seeking safety and escaping conflict, persecution, and humanitarian crises.
As of August 2024, displacement flows into Southeast Asia are driven largely by crises in Asia and Africa, with Myanmar representing the single largest source of refugees. Smaller movements also emerge from regions such as Europe (Russia) and Oceania (Australia and Fiji), reflecting that displacement into Malaysia is not only regional but global.

02. Demographics

A Voice for the Rohingya

The Rohingya constitute the largest refugee community in Malaysia, arriving after decades of persecution and statelessness. Although Malaysia hosts significant refugee populations, the government does not operate a formal asylum system. As a result, refugees and asylum-seekers can be only registered and supported primarily through the UNHCR.

The presence of a large undocumented refugee population has affected local social and economic tensions, shaping public discourse and local resentment over time. At the same time, the Rohingya community remains facing limited access to legal employment, education, and social protection, leaving many in precarious circumstances

The failure of handling refugees and undocumented migrants underscore the need for shared responsibility and long-term solutions to displacement within the region.

04. Status

Refugees & Stateless Persons

Malaysia continues to see a steady rise in people registered with the UNHCR, including both refugees and asylum-seekers. The numbers have increased significantly over the past two decades, reflecting ongoing regional instability and prolonged displacement.
